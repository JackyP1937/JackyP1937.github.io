Jack Peters

I'm a robotics engineer and Ph.D. candidate at Vanderbilt University, where I work in the MEDLab and C* Controls Lab on the design, modeling, and control of robotic systems. My research focuses on continuum robotics, including MRI-compatible robots for minimally invasive neurosurgery and modern control methods for soft robots. I really enjoy exploring the different ways in which we interact with and control robotic systems and testing and refining those algorithms on hardware I've built.

Before graduate school, I worked as an electrical design engineer at ASML, developing mechatronic systems for semiconductor photolithography machines. Before that, I was at Medtronic working on the Hugo™ robot-assisted surgery system and was an engineering student at the University of Connecticut.

I hold an M.S. in Mechanical Engineering from Vanderbilt University and a B.S.E. from the University of Connecticut, where I double majored in Electrical Engineering and Biomedical Engineering. I will defend my Ph.D. dissertation in Mechanical Engineering in December 2026.

Selected Work

 An MRI-Guided Robotic System to Improve Hippocampal Access for Epilepsy Interventions
John E. Peters, Abby M. Grillo, Mahshid Mansouri, Daniel S. Esser, Sarah Garrow, Nithin S. Kumar, William A. Grissom, Dario J. Englot, Joseph S. Neimat, Robert J. Webster III, Eric J. Barth
Annals of Biomedical Engineering. In review.
arXiv:2609.13572

Designed and experimentally validated a compact 6-DOF, MRI-compatible robotic system for minimally invasive neurosurgery. This system was designed to improval hippocampal access, a common seizure source in drug-resistant epilepsy, by delivering a curved needle-like laser ablator through a natural opening in the skull base. Both the delivery path and the curved nature of the needle improve upon current clinical straight-line laser interstitial thermal therapy (LITT) as measured by hippocampal cannulation percentage. In MRI-guided experiments, we achieved a mean cannulation percentage of 83.5% exceeding reported clinical averages of 50-60%. Seizure control is believed by physicians to be a function of hippocampal volume treated, and our system provides a means of treating a greater volume of the hippocampus with LITT-based interventions.

 Conforming to Curvature: Improving Hippocampal Ablation Coverage with Robotic, Curvilinear, Directional MRgLITT
John E. Peters, Nithin S. Kumar, Abby Grillo, Mahshid Mansouri, Saikat Sengupta, Sarah Garrow, Kerrin Sunshine, William A. Grissom, Robert J. Webster III, Eric J. Barth
IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering. In preparation.

In this work we extended the neurosurgical robot from curved needle delivery to conformal therapy by integrating a directional laser probe and real-time MR thermometry monitoring. This enabled us to achieve 84.2% mean hippocampal ablation coverage versus the reported 56% clinical average in tissue-mimicking phantoms.

 Constrained Lagrangian-Based, Nonholonomic Modeling and Validation of a Pneumatically Actuated Wheeled Soft Robot
Nithin S. Kumar, John E. Peters, Sung Y. Kim, Joshua Gaston, D. Caleb Rucker, Eric J. Barth
Journal of Mechanisms and Robotics. Published. 2026.

In this work, we designed a pneumatically actuated, wheeled soft robot and a constrained, Lagrangian-based model to accurately capture its highly dynamic, non-holonomic locomotion. The robot achieved speeds up to 22 cm/s (1.38 body lengths/s) and demonstrated forward, reverse, and circular locomotion. The modeling framework was validated across three different designs, accurately reproducing their experimentally observed configuration and velocity trends.

 Open-Source Concentric Tube Robot Simulation & Teleoperation
John E. Peters.
Independent Project · Python · ROS2 · Git
GitHub

In this work, I developed an open-source simulation and control framework for concentric tube robots using ROS2/Python. This involved implementation of Cosserat-rod Newtonian mechanics, tube-torsion modeling, and kinematic control. Real-time task-space teleoperation using an Xbox controller was achieved using a resolved-rates/IK approach and online, warm-started Jacobian computation. The robot is visualized in RViz as the user inputs tip velocity commands via the controller joystick. I organized the simulator as a modular ROS2 package separating out mechanics, kinematics, controls, and visualization, and included automated testing as well as Git version control.

 Data-driven Dynamic Modeling and Control of Pneumatic Soft Actuators with Stability Analysis
Nithin S. Kumar, John E. Peters, Thomas Beckers, Eric J. Barth.
ASME Letters in Dynamic Systems and Control. Accepted. 2026.

Continuum robots offer inherent compliance and dexterity, but their nonlinear deformation and coupled actuation make accurate modeling and task-space control challenging. This work developed a data-driven modeling and control framework for pneumatically actuated, soft robots. Using experimentally learned, nonlinear feedforward maps combined with EDMDc residual dynamics we achieved 2 mm RMSE positional control of the robot tip. This was demonstrated on shape rich trajectories with less than 10 mm RMSE at tip speeds exceeding 100 mm/s. The learned model also enables closed-loop stability prediction without requiring an explicit first-principles dynamic model.

 Real-Time Data-Driven Task-Space Control of Tendon-Drive Continuum Robots
Nithin S. Kumar, John E. Peters, Eric J. Barth.
ASME/IEEE Transactions on Mechatronics. In preparation.

This work extended our previous data-driven approach to 3D task space control of tendon-driven continuum robots. A novel modeling and control framework was developed for these robots, learning static mappings from actuator commands to tip position and orientation from just 600 s of experimental data. The learned models are exploited for optimization-based feedforward inversion and Jacobian feedback, enabling real-time millimeter-scale tracking without an explicit mechanics-based model. These results demonstrate that learned static input-output models provide an accurate and computationally efficient foundation for real-time continuum-robot task-space control.
